# PRISM Research Principles

Generated: 2026-07-28

PRISM optimizes for reproducible predictive improvement, not attractive hindsight narratives.

## Core principles

- Freeze predictions before kickoff and preserve their original evidence state.
- Separate prediction generation from outcome ingestion.
- Evaluate production and candidate versions on the same governed cohort.
- Require enough real forward cases before promotion; insufficient evidence means HOLD, not assumed success.
- Preserve exact-score performance as a first-class layer while also evaluating full-stack decision quality.
- Treat venue regime, competitive readiness, personnel regime breaks and scenario uncertainty explicitly.
- Prevent market information from being counted multiple times through correlated representations.
- Distinguish confirmed facts from projected events and scenario-weight projections rather than applying them deterministically.
- Prefer calibrated probabilities and error decomposition over anecdotal success/failure explanations.
- Every promotion decision must be reproducible from immutable prediction/outcome evidence and identifiable dataset provenance.
- Failed predictions are training evidence. Never remove inconvenient cases from the governed cohort.
- Changes to model behaviour require regression coverage and CI enforcement before becoming trusted architecture.
